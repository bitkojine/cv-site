# Why I Left LinkedIn for GitHub

After half a year of actively posting about Bitcoin on LinkedIn, I woke up one morning to find my account blocked. No warning. No explanation. Just locked out.

## What Happened

I'd been sharing my thoughts on Bitcoin, decentralization, and financial sovereignty. Nothing illegal. Nothing hateful. Just my perspective on technology that I genuinely believe will change the world.

LinkedIn apparently disagreed. Or maybe their algorithm did. Either way, **six months of posts, connections, and professional history — gone**.

## The Problem with Platforms

Social platforms give you reach, but they take away ownership. When you build your professional presence on someone else's land:

- **They control your content** — What you can say, how you can say it
- **They control your audience** — Your connections aren't really yours
- **They control your history** — Years of writing can vanish in a click
- **They have no accountability** — Good luck getting a human to review your case

## My Solution: Self-Hosting on GitHub

This blog runs on my own domain, hosted via GitHub Pages. The code lives in my Git repository. Here's what that means:

1. **I own my content** — Every post is a markdown file I control
2. **No censorship risk** — Nobody can deplatform my own website
3. **Permanent URLs** — My articles won't break when a company pivots
4. **Full transparency** — You can literally see the [source code](https://github.com/bitkojine/cv-site)
5. **Version history** — Every change is tracked, nothing is lost

## The Irony

The very technology LinkedIn blocked me for discussing — decentralization and censorship resistance — is exactly why I can now publish freely.

Bitcoin taught me that **"not your keys, not your coins"** applies beyond money. It applies to your data, your content, your professional identity.

**Not your server, not your content.**

## Moving Forward

I'm not bitter about LinkedIn. It's a company doing what companies do — managing risk and protecting their platform. I get it.

But I've learned my lesson. From now on:

- My long-form content lives here, on my own domain
- My code and projects live on GitHub
- My professional identity isn't dependent on any single platform

If you want to follow my writing, bookmark [robertasrudys.com/blog](/blog). No algorithm. No account required. Just content.
