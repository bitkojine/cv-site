# Why I Left LinkedIn for GitHub

For about six months I posted regularly on LinkedIn, mostly about Bitcoin and decentralization. Then one day I woke up to a locked account. No warning, no clear explanation, just a message that access was restricted.

I do not claim the platform was wrong. Platforms are complex and moderation is messy. Still, the experience made something painfully obvious: if a platform can turn off access overnight, then it is not a place to store years of work.

## The Real Issue

Social networks are great for reach. They are not great for ownership. When you build your professional voice on someone else’s platform, you accept their rules, their algorithms, and their uncertainty.

## What I Chose Instead

I moved my long‑form writing to my own site, hosted from a Git repository. That gives me a few guarantees that matter to me:

- I keep the source files and the history
- the URLs are mine and can stay stable
- I can move hosts without breaking the archive
- I can publish without asking for permission

The irony is that decentralization is exactly what makes this possible. The same ideas that drew me to Bitcoin also pointed me toward self‑hosting.

A phrase I keep in mind is simple: not your server, not your content.

## Moving Forward

I am not trying to pick a fight with LinkedIn. It did what large platforms do. I just learned where I want my writing to live.
